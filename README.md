## 📁 Featured Projects

These are **self-directed portfolio projects using fictional organizations and synthetic data**. They demonstrate methods and working artifacts; regulatory mappings are learning exercises and do not represent client compliance conclusions.

### [NorthStar Digital Bank — Security Compliance Operations Engine](https://github.com/prasanna-sriram/northstar-compliance-operations-engine)

Built a compliance operations model connecting controls, evidence, policies, risks, vendors, and privacy requests for a fictional digital bank. **Seven Python scripts** automate evidence-freshness checks, policy reviews, remediation aging, control summaries, risk scoring, vendor tiering, and privacy-request monitoring. The tiering engine identified **four tier-drift cases**, while the privacy workflow flagged **four of 30 requests** as overdue. A **five-page Power BI dashboard**, evidence index, and security review pack bring the results together for operational follow-up and assurance discussions. The project explores ISO 27001, SOC 2, and PIPEDA-related control concepts.

**Skills demonstrated:** Python automation, Power BI/DAX, evidence management, vendor risk scoring, privacy workflow monitoring, cross-framework mapping.

### [Continuous IT GRC Monitoring for SaaS Platforms — NetWave](https://github.com/prasanna-sriram/netwave-grc-monitoring)

Built a repeatable control-monitoring and evidence pipeline for a fictional SaaS company. A SQL Server schema and **11 parameterized checks** identify excessive privileges, dormant privileged accounts, terminated-user access, missing MFA, unapproved changes, and SoD conflicts. A Python runner produces dated CSV evidence and a run manifest that distinguishes **zero findings from failed checks**, preserving visibility when execution breaks. A **four-page Power BI report** supports executive review, access governance, change/SoD analysis, and departmental follow-up. The architecture includes conceptual AWS/Azure extension paths for future cloud IAM monitoring.

**Skills demonstrated:** SQL Server, Python evidence automation, access governance, SOX ITGC concepts, SoD analysis, Power BI/DAX.

### [AuroraPay — Product Risk Analytics for Real-Time Payments](https://github.com/prasanna-sriram/aurorapay-product-risk-case-study)

Designed a **second-line ERM simulation** for a fictional Canadian payments network. Built a **12-item risk register across five themes** and analyzed approximately **100,000 synthetic transactions** to calculate volume-normalized fraud and operational KRIs. The analysis compared payment-rail exposure and connected privileged-access and vendor-resilience gaps to broader product risks. A Power BI executive dashboard and risk paper turn those findings into **three prioritized, time-bound recommendations**. The project demonstrates how assessment, appetite statements, and analytical challenge can support product decisions and remediation discussions while making the scenario assumptions explicit.

**Skills demonstrated:** Product risk assessment, risk registers, appetite statements, Python/Pandas, KRI design, Power BI, executive risk reporting.

### [Payments Risk 360 — Enterprise Risk & AI-Driven Fraud Detection](https://github.com/prasanna-sriram/payments-risk-360)

Built a synthetic Canadian payments scenario connecting **four workstreams**: PD-style participant credit scoring, intraday liquidity stress testing, rules-based fraud detection, and explainable logistic-regression fraud scoring. Python models explore distress indicators, concentration, transaction patterns, and time-to-breach thresholds under baseline and stressed conditions. An integrated Power BI dashboard combines results with RCSA-style documentation, KRIs, escalation logic, and second-line challenge notes. Conceptual regulatory alignment notes explore Canadian payments expectations. The project extends my governance and controls portfolio into quantitative risk analysis and model interpretation through a simulated environment.

**Skills demonstrated:** Python/scikit-learn, credit-risk simulation, liquidity stress testing, fraud analytics, model explainability, Power BI, RCSA-style documentation.

## 🧠 What I'm Working On

I'm developing a **Canadian risk case-study series** across technology and third-party risk, operational resilience, AI governance, and privacy. The work combines a refresh of frameworks I've used with study of Canadian financial-services expectations and newer governance topics.

Each series follows a fictional organization through practical decisions: **what could go wrong, what evidence to inspect, who should decide, and what remains unresolved**. Chapters will be accompanied by worked examples, reusable templates, and source references, with shorter reflections shared on LinkedIn.

**Current stage:** Preparing the first technology and third-party risk chapter and its accompanying post. The four repositories have starter materials; no case-study chapters have been published yet.

### [The Integration Deadline — Technology and Third-Party Risk](TECHNOLOGY_TPRM_REPOSITORY_URL)

Follows **Alderbank**, a fictional bank assessing an analytics integration under delivery pressure. Explores service ownership, vendor access, data flows, due diligence, onboarding conditions, and monitoring reliability. The learning connects **OSFI B-10 and B-13** with familiar NIST CSF, ISO 27001, SOC reporting, SOX/ICFR, and PCI DSS concepts where relevant.

### [The Day the Workaround Failed — Operational Resilience](OPERATIONAL_RESILIENCE_REPOSITORY_URL)

Follows **Cedarline Assurance**, a fictional insurer testing whether its fallback arrangements can sustain important operations. Explores critical operations, dependencies, disruption tolerances, recovery assumptions, and scenario testing. The focus is the **evidence behind recovery claims**, including whether manual workarounds preserve capacity, accountability, and control.

### [The Assistant with Too Much Authority — AI Governance](AI_GOVERNANCE_REPOSITORY_URL)

Follows **Northglass Life**, a fictional life insurer introducing an AI assistant. Explores the boundary between assistance and decision-making, human oversight, use-case risk assessment, evaluation evidence, and accountability. Uses **NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, and ISO/IEC 23894** as learning references for examining governance choices.

### [The Data Nobody Meant to Keep — Privacy Governance](PRIVACY_GOVERNANCE_REPOSITORY_URL)

Follows **HarbourPay**, a fictional payment service provider reviewing its data practices. Explores privacy impact assessment, data minimization, retention, third-party sharing, cross-border access, and privacy risks involving AI. The focus is translating **Canadian privacy requirements and privacy-by-design principles** into practical questions, decisions, and evidence.
